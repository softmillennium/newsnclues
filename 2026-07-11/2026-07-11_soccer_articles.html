No team blends soccer's central duality better: the tension between flair and brutality, between honoring the game and seeing what you can get away with in order to win. Four years ago, Lionel Messi, soccer's greatest ever player, won the World Cup with Argentina in his fifth attempt, erasing the only blemish in his incredible career with a starring role in what was called the best final of all time. Argentina is an enigma of a team centered around Messi and the religious fervor he inspires — plus, some suspect, occasional help from the officials. Suddenly, what was charming four years ago looks questionable. As they square off in the quarterfinals on Saturday against Switzerland, Argentina is, strangely enough, the closest thing this World Cup has to a villain. As in every game he has played at this tournament, Messi spent most of it walking. Walking has accounted for 63 per cent of his movement across the World Cup, a share that is far clear of any other outfield player in the competition. And even these strolls were among his more energetic movements, with Messi spending a further 25 per cent of the match simply standing still. It is tempting to put this down to age: a 39-year-old carefully managing his body, conserving his energy for the most decisive moments.
