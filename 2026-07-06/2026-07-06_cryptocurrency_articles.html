The interaction of blockchain technologies with additive manufacture (AM) processes enabled by IoT, specifically VAT Photopolymerization (VPP), offers innovative means for increased security, transparency, and traceability in industrial production data. The VPP-IoT system discussed in the present study is based on sensor data for monitoring and mitigating VOC emissions. To ensure secure flow and management of sensor data in this system, we implemented a tamper-proof and transparent access system for stakeholders based on blockchain-decentralized and immutable technology. Hyperledger Fabric was used to set up a private blockchain network that worked with IoT sensors to keep track of VOC emissions in real time. Smart contracts were designed to automate data validation, storage, and alert generation processes to maintain compliance with environmental and safety regulations. The blockchain-based system demonstrated high-performance metrics, such as an average transaction throughput of 487.2 tps and data integrity verification success of 99.99%. The results point toward blockchain-IoT integration's potential to improve regulatory compliance, predictive maintenance, and real-time monitoring in VPP additive manufacturing for safer and more sustainable industrial practices. While experimental results established a strong positive correlation between VOC emissions and key printing parameters, such as exposure duration and light intensity, layer thickness showed little effect. This study has broader implications as it promotes the application of Industry 4.0 concepts in a more practical context for VPP additive manufacturing. Additive manufacturing (AM) has become a defining technology of Industry 4.0 by changing how complex, detailed, and accurate parts are made. These parts are used in many industries that need custom products, like biomedical, aerospace, and consumer goods. VPP, which includes techniques such as stereolithography, DLP, etc., works on the concept of curing photopolymer resins layer-wise using UV light directly from digital models to produce parts with exceptional dimensional accuracy and surface finish1,2. These emissions are harmful, and they compromise workplace air quality for operators. One of the innovative solutions that surfaced in recent literature is the adoption of real-time monitoring and mitigation strategies achieved by integrating IoT sensors into VPP to ensure safe and sustainable manufacturing practices3,4,5,6,7. However, these integrated systems open the possibilities for new opportunities, as well as challenges that require immediate attention. On one hand, VOC detection and air quality monitoring during VPP deliver process optimization, effective control, and reduced harmful emissions. On the other, the integrated IoT devices generate immense amounts of data, raising concerns about data security, integrity, and transparency. This brings us to the central concern this research revolves around: the need for robust mechanisms that could guarantee the security of data transmission from point to point within VPP-IoT-enabled systems. Sensor data that varies from VOC measurements, resin temperature, etc., must be stored and managed safely with a high level of reliability and traceability. Without such features in place, the integrity of this data can lose dependability, which may lead to inadequate verification procedures, non-conformity to regulation, and potentially unprecedented dangers. Specifically, compromised VOC data could negatively influence the selection of ventilation measures, potentially exposing the worker environment to hazardous conditions8,9. With the immutable and decentralized framework, blockchain has evolved into a strong tool implemented across various sectors, including healthcare, smart energy systems, and supply chain management, going beyond how it was originally perceived as a foundation for digital currency10. Within the context of AM, blockchain exists as a distributed ledger that manages stored sensor data while ensuring security, traceability, and transparency. In other words, when blockchain is implemented in VPP-IoT systems, it will be possible for manufacturers to embrace higher data integrity so that the stakeholders can be provided with quality data that can be relied on11,12. Many of the topics focused on architecture15, supply chain management16,17 , enhancing security, and transparency18,19,20. Others recognized the benefits of blockchain in safeguarding sensitive digital design assets and intellectual property rights. Through its encrypted structure, blockchain ensures that records of design remain tamper-proof and accessible only by authorized personnel. Besides immutable record storing, blockchain decentralized architecture permits peer-to-peer collaboration among manufacturers, thereby facilitating secure exchange of manufacturing resources. Such frameworks are largely utilized in aerospace and automotive sectors15,21. A recurring study also reported that auditable materials, process parameters, and component records can be documented in blockchain in a way that each manufacturing procedure is traced. This traceability supports product tracking and reinforces quality assurance, building trust among stakeholders and minimizing the risk of substandard parts. A relevant example can be found in the biomedical industry, where blockchain verifies that materials used to fabricate implants meet the requirements prescribed and standards. Moreover, other common themes include the automation of different aspects of the AM workflow using smart contracts, according to the literature review conducted by Ferreira et al. in 202422 and Kumar et al. in 202323, with supplemental studies by Westphal et al. in 202324. Smart contracts are self-executing agreements stored on the blockchain to automate processes and transactions25. Their utility is helpful in cases where task execution and control conditions are predefined, so the blockchain performs interventions without regular human access and permission. Beyond automation, blockchain also offers lifecycle management of digital twins' systems operations and enhances their reliability and traceability26. Despite the significant advances in the implementation of blockchain technology in AM across domains, comprising supply chain, intellectual property protection, digital part management, and integration within digital twins' frameworks. These studies remain conceptual or simulation-based, and they overlook the critical need for real-time, reliable monitoring of workspace environmental condition data within AM operations. Specifically, no experimental investigation to date has demonstrated the capability of a blockchain-based IoT system to track and validate VOC emissions in VPP AM, aiming to enhance operational safety and data transparency. The present study addresses the gap by equipping a VPP-IoT-based system with a private Hyperledger Fabric network (PBFT) for secure and transparent real-time VOC monitoring. Using smart contract validation, AES-256 encryption, and automated alerts, the proposed framework seeks to deliver an environmentally accountable data management infrastructure for VPP AM. A multidisciplinary approach combining experimental studies with IoT-enabled VPP systems, blockchain development, and data analytics is adopted in this paper. The setup consists of IoT sensors enabling VOC emission tracking, resin temperature and environmental condition monitoring, and a blockchain-based secure data recording and sharing platform. The framework continues to investigate the use of smart contracts in automating data validation and ensuring that set rules are followed. The novelty of this research lies in its experimental realization of blockchain IoT-based integration within a VPP environment. Unlike most previous works that propose conceptual or simulated architectures, this study demonstrates a fully implemented Hyperledger Fabric network with PBFT consensus connected to IoT sensors for real-time VOC emission monitoring. The framework introduces a smart contract validation and alert system that enforces environmental and safety compliance autonomously. Furthermore, the quantitative benchmarking of blockchain performance establishes an empirical foundation for scalable, sustainable, and regulation-aligned adoption of blockchain-enabled VPP-AM. Blockchain's suitability and efficiency as a technology for widespread industrial applications is a significant challenge. Many blockchain platforms rely on consensus mechanisms, which can be computationally expensive and reduce the transactions per second; thus, building a blockchain system with the capability to address these obstacles within the context of VPP AM is essential27,28. Additionally, challenges arise from the compatibility of VPP infrastructure with the different software applications and hardware instruments to be incorporated during the integration process. This issue often leads to the emergence of complexities in how blockchain-based solutions are implemented and managed since there are often no well-defined protocols to follow and because blockchain platforms are constantly undergoing development22,29. This study is critical because the benefits are likely to extend beyond the laboratory and be realized in the transformation of monitoring and control of manufacturing processes in the context of AM. The insights gained in this research may contribute to the development of industry-wide best practices for standardizing blockchain integration into AM technologies. This research attempts to contribute to the progress of smart manufacturing and the realization of Industry 4.0 by addressing these challenges while ensuring safety and sustainability for AM processes13,14. Being an evolving field, the findings of this research are valuable for informing future innovations and shaping the future of industrial manufacturing. This section provides a description of how blockchain can be applied to the IoT-VPP AM System to enhance data security, trust, and traceability. The experimental setup has been made to provide an approach towards a VPP-AM setup in which a significant focus is being given to monitoring the VOC emissions, along with other paramount process parameters, during the experiments. The VPP-AM machine, which is a high-resolution Anycubic Photon Mono X 6 K with a 9.25-inch monochrome exposure screen, was used to conduct experiments. A set of IoT sensors was connected to the experimental device to measure VOC emissions, resin temperature, and environmental conditions. VOC Sensor: A Sparkfun SGP30 Air Quality Sensor Breakout (Qwiic) was used to measure and determine VOC emissions throughout printing. The sensor reports the readings of total VOCs (TVOC) in parts per billion (ppb), carbon dioxide (CO₂) equivalent in parts per million (ppm), and H₂. All the fabrication tests were undertaken in closed chambers at 23 ± 1 °C and 50 ± 2% RH. Randomization of print orders and resin-exposure combinations between experimental runs was used to eliminate systematic bias. Each trial was preceded by a 2-hour baseline, which was used to establish VOC balance and ambient stability. The sensor values have been recorded at a rate of 1 Hz, and they have been averaged with a three-sample moving average filter to reduce short-term filter changes. The minicomputer used as a central data acquisition and processing control unit was Raspberry Pi 4. The sensors communicated both through their PCI connector and I2C communication to the board, where they provided real-time data at set times. The data obtained was recorded and put aside to be analyzed later. The accuracy of each sensor was ensured by calibrating it with laboratory reference instruments before data were collected. The preprocessing script in Raspberry Pi 4 used calibration coefficients automatically. We used a commercially produced grey photopolymer resin in all our experiments to ensure that we have similarity in the material properties. The reason why we selected it is that it is compatible with the Anycubic Photon Mono X 6 K and is commonly applied in industries. All procedures conducted during the experiments adhered to institutional safety regulations and OSHA allowable exposures to VOC. The experimental condition was to be under inspection of forced ventilation (12 ACH air exchange rate), and personnel must be equipped with proper equilibrium personal protection. None of the personal identifiable information (PII) or data of workers is collected, logged, and transferred. The private Hyperledger Fabric network uses membership service providers to control access based on roles, allowing only certain authorized organizational nodes (like machine operators, quality control personnel, and compliance officers) to view ledger data based on what they need for their jobs. The blockchain architecture was developed to ensure secure and transparent data management in the VPP system that is enabled for IoT. It consists of three main layers (Fig. 3): the data acquisition, blockchain, and application layers. Blockchain-based IoT architecture for VOC monitoring in VPP-AM. Data is generated by the IoT sensor array observing the VPP process, ultimately acquired and pre-processed by the Raspberry Pi 4 edge device, encrypted using AES-256, and created as a transaction to the Hyperledger Fabric network. Lastly, the application layer provides stakeholders with real-time monitoring and automated alerts through a web dashboard. The data acquisition layer consists of IoT sensors collecting real-time data on VOC emissions, resin temperature, and environmental conditions. The Hyperledger Fabric framework was recently adopted to create a private blockchain network due to its scalability and modular optimization, which allows it to support smart contracts30. The distributed ledger is stored in each node. Importantly, the contracts also impose preconditions, such as sensor calibration and data integrity checks, prior to significantly committing any records to the immutable ledger. PBFT has been selected since it is suitable in a private blockchain setting in cases where the trust of parties is comparatively low32. The Hyperledger Fabric architecture was set up with Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) consensus among three validating peers (one ordering node and two endorsing peers). Each block was formed after every 500 ms with a maximum of 50 transactions in each block. Although the full IoT load (4 sensor nodes transmitting 1 Hz) was applied to this network, the network was able to sustain an average throughput of 487.2 tps with an average block finalization latency of 1.18 s. The early-stage throughput value, which ranged from 430 to 450 tps, was associated with the pre-optimization tests conducted prior to changes in the batch timeout and endorsement policy. Snapshot of the chaincode implementation for VOC data validation and secure ledger storage in Hyperledger Fabric. The code segment illustrates the automated smart contract functions that verify sensor calibration, check for outlier readings, and validate permissible VOC (ppb) thresholds before data commitment. Each validated record is cryptographically hashed and appended to the distributed ledger under PBFT consensus, ensuring immutability and auditability. Moreover, the blockchain was evaluated for network performance using Hyperledger Caliper (v0.5.0), which provided performance indicators for throughput, latency, and consensus performance based on a sustained ingestion of IoT readouts from Raspberry Pi 4. Each performance measurement is the mean of three experimental replications, given a constant device sample rate of 5 Hz. The application layer provides a user interface for accessing and interacting with data stored on the blockchain. A dashboard has been developed using React.js, enabling users to view emissions, temperature, and humidity in real time. Alerts are shown on the dashboard when VOC levels are above a permissible limit so that operators can act in time. Data is preprocessed before inserting into the blockchain to ensure both correctness and uniformity. The Raspberry Pi 4 aggregates sensor data and performs preliminary validation, including calibration verification and outlier detection. This preprocessed data is subsequently formatted into a JSON structure standardized for storage in the blockchain. All data were encrypted with AES-256 encryption and were sent to the blockchain network to enhance the protection of data. To guarantee safe passage of information, AES-256 encryption was utilized with a dynamically selected session key that was generated in a key-generation procedure using SHA-256. The code of the encryption key was stored in the local secure storage of Raspberry Pi to be refreshed every now and then to eliminate replay/compromise. The sensor payloads were encrypted and hashed with SHA-256. To help limit access to schedules and timestamps of the ledger, Hyperledger Fabric Chaincode added access control through vaults. This solution ensured the integrity of data through blockchain technology and ledger control. The records are matched with a time stamp, and each one connects to the previous record, creating an indestructible chain of records. The introduction of latency and energy overhead caused by the encryption pipeline and blockchain storage pipeline was parameterized and systematically quantified as to their practical feasibility of operation in real time. Smart contracts play a critical role in automating data validation and storage. For example, our VOC validation contract verifies that sensor readings fall within predefined safety thresholds before blockchain commitment. If validation fails, the contract generates an alert and suspends data recording until the issue is resolved. The completed blockchain network on the Hyperledger Fabric performed well at processing sensor data when used in IoT. The blockchain network exhibits empirical performance measures, which are obtained with the help of Hyperledger Caliper in the exchange of data in real time with the IoT sensors, as shown in Table 2. During the entire duration of high data ingestion, there was an average of 487.2 tps in the system. This rate of throughput was normally adequate to process the real-time sensor data during the VPP process. During its peak performance, it has an average of around 180 transactions per minute. To prove the feasibility of IoT data acquisition and blockchain storage, we evaluated the end-to-end latency of 1,000 sensor readings for ledger commitment transactions (Table 3). The cumulative latency of 441.0 ± 45.6 ms was not much further than the block creation interval of 2.1 s, which ensured real-time monitoring possibilities. Energy profiling showed that the Raspberry Pi 4 used 4.8 W (1.9% of system power), and blockchain metadata was 387 bytes / 512-byte payload. These findings support the conclusion that blockchain-IoT integration will minimize overhead while maintaining data integrity and real-time performance. Displaying a time-series analysis of VOC emissions achieved during VPP-AM has indicated several distinctive patterns and trends that can serve as a clue to the dynamics of VOC emissions. VOC (ppb) spiked quickly in the VPP process; in a few minutes the levels had a higher value in the region of 2900 ppb. The spike was experienced under the subsequent parameters of the process: exposure time of 2 s, light intensity at 100%, and a layer thickness of 50 m. This sudden rise in VOC may be due to the curing of the bottom layer, which has a longer exposure period compared to the normal exposure period. After the initial surge, VOC levels stabilized, indicating that the initial layers play a significant role in VOC emissions. While printing, it was noticeable that the VOC (ppb) was increasing slowly and nearly in a linear fashion following the initial stabilization in the graph. This difference would indicate that VOCs are emitted during printing, but at a slower rate compared to the initial burst. As a result of this spike, the levels of VOCs would gradually grow during the printing procedure, smaller than initially. The progressively higher VOC could be attributed to the accrued nature of a continuous polymerization reaction and cured resin over a specified period. The suggested blockchain-IoT combination introduced in the article refers to the different fields of application of current blockchain-AM studies on supply chain traceability (Alkhader et al., 2020), IP protection (Chan et al., 2023), and digital twins (Mandolla et al., 2019). They are integrated at the enterprise level; we have blockchain at the process control layer to measure real-time VOC emissions and environmental data during fabrication. The delivered performance of Hyperledger Fabric, at 487.2 transactions per second (tps), is 16 to 32 times greater than that of Ethereum frameworks, which operate at 15 to 30 tps, and offers sub-second latency for real-time monitoring as reported by Kumar et al. (2023). However, we sacrifice radical decentralization to achieve deterministic finality suitable for regulated consortia. With 3.2% false and 96.8% alert accuracy with 127 alerts, our smart contracts showed operational maturity beyond conceptual frameworks that do not have empirical measures (Westphal et al., 2023). The operational value of blockchain in comparison with its architectural novelty is seen in the comparative validations (Table 9). Organizations benefit from immutability during regulatory audits, as evidenced by a 9.6 times greater tampering detection rate for perfect systems compared to a rate of 13.0 for centralized systems. Additionally, the advantages of automation, which include a 99.3 times faster alert response, surpass those of human-dependent workflows in safety-critical environments. The power usage of the private Hyperledger Fabric estimated network was 0.003–0.006 kWh per 1,000 requests, which was less than 0.15 kWh in all experiments. Conversely, monitoring enabled using blockchain demonstrated 18.25% VOC emission reductions, which shows that the low energy footprint of blockchain can be compensated by significant environmental benefits. Easy scaling with the assistance of additional VPP printers is also supported by the architecture since the Hyperledger Fabric network can include new peers through standardized MQTT interfaces. Smart contracts ensure that all devices have data validation that is the same, and private channels and endorsement policies allow a safe interoperability system between a range of machines or facilities within industries. There are essential constraints that should be investigated. The four-node PBFT network implemented (f = 1) offers only partial resilience; with production at 7–13 nodes, consensus latency is O(n²), or about 178.4 ms, with the PBFT network's (n 2) complexity. Energy use (78 kWh/45 days, 2.4 overhead) is proportional to nodes. Scalability has not been tested beyond single-machine monitoring (487.2 tps), and it is theorized to support 80–160 machines with on-chain storage, although it reached a performance of 2.3 GB/45 days, which requires off-chain solutions (IPFS). Limited generalizability: This system can only utilize a restricted number of resins on a single machine, and it operates under specific parameters. VCs may vary between different systems and chemistries. Possible future research directions are as follows: (1) federated architecture balancing transparency and confidentiality of multi-organization networks; (2) blockchain-authenticated data integration alongside ML-based predictive VOC control; (3) extension to other parameters (viscosity, quality metrics) to create omnipresent digital signatures; (4) discussion of energy-conscious blockchain applications, including proof-of-stake and DAG-based applications, to ensure scalability and lessen the operational footprint. New EU rules create compliance incentives for audible environmental records, serving as a distinct motivation separate from counterfeiting. Powder bed fusion and extrusion, in turn, have implications for industry, which may make blockchain a standard of safety-critical AM. This study has expressed the possibility of integrating blockchain technology with IoT-enabled VPP-AM systems to address some of the most formidable problems in the current generation in terms of producing secure, transparent, and traceable data. Smart contracts and Hyperledger Fabric enabled the real-time validation of data, automatization of alerts, and adherence to environmental standards, a solid solution in the context of industrial manufacturing. The experimental evidence revealed that the significant positive correlation between VOC emissions and the key printing settings, exposure time, and light intensity was noted, whereas the layer thickness did not have a significant impact. This information is beneficial for streamlining VPP processes, leading to lower harmful emissions while maintaining print quality. The IoT-based system demonstrated strong high-performance indicators, including a transaction throughput of 487.2 tps and a data integrity success rate of 99.99%, thereby showcasing its stability and scalability for the industrial sector. The research has more implications for future AM and Industry 4.0. The proposed system will be scaled to incorporate other crucial parameters in future work, like the viscosity and efficiency of resin cures. These could be improved in a future study, and alternative studies conducted on energy-efficient consensus mechanisms that may augment blockchain-based solutions more on sustainability.
